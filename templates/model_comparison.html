Why the differences?

Logistic Regression

Combines scaled activity measures into a straight-line risk estimate.

It is simple to interpret but may miss complex interactions.

K-Nearest Neighbors

Looks at students with similar activity patterns.

It can catch local patterns but depends on the chosen number of neighbors.

Decision Tree

Uses a sequence of yes-or-no splits.

It is easy to explain but one tree can be sensitive to small data changes.

Random Forest

Averages predictions from many decision trees.

It can handle interactions, though its decisions are harder to trace directly.

The dataset is simulated and its labels were generated from a formula, so these rankings mainly show how each method behaves on this data. Small differences between models are not strong evidence, and real data may rank them differently.
